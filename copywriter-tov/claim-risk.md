# Claim risk reference (EU / France)

Used by the Claim check. It informs recommendations and never blocks copy. It is a working summary, not legal advice: the outcome depends on the exact product, wording, evidence held and context, and the texts change. For regulated products, have final copy reviewed by a specialist before publishing. Last reviewed: November 2026.

## Risk levels

- **Low:** obvious puffery about experience or feeling ("the best morning ritual", "exceptional quality", "unmatched taste"). EU law tolerates exaggerated statements not meant to be taken literally (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC, art. 5(3)). It moves up a level as soon as it sounds measurable or names a rival.
- **Medium:** a claim a reader could take as a checkable fact, a comparison, or wording with no legal definition that can mislead ("natural", "pure").
- **High:** claims governed by sector rules (health, therapeutic, environmental, organic), or claims that are unlawful when unproven or false (rankings, test results, guarantees, prices, reviews, urgency).

In France, misleading commercial practices (Code de la consommation, L121-2 and following) are enforced by the DGCCRF and carry criminal and administrative penalties.

## By claim type (every sector)

| Claim | Risk | Why | Safer route |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Best ever", "unmatched", "exceptional", "game-changing" about the experience | Low | Puffery: not meant literally. | Keep. |
| Unnamed comparison ("nothing else comes close") | Low–medium | Usually puffery; becomes a factual comparison if it adds numbers or a category. | Keep it unmeasured. |
| "No. 1", "best-selling", "leader", "le plus vendu", "préféré des Français" | High | Read as a fact: it needs a source, date and scope you can show. | Add source, date and scope, or turn it into experience ("our most-loved blend"). |
| Comparison that names or clearly identifies a competitor | High | Comparative advertising must compare like with like, on objective, verifiable features, without denigration (Code de la consommation L122-1 and following; Directive 2006/114/EC). | Compare to an unnamed alternative, or compare one verifiable feature. |
| Measurable performance ("lasts 12 h", "absorbs in 30 s", "clinically proven", "dermatologically tested", "-30 % wrinkles") | High unless the brief cites the test | The evidence must exist before publishing. | Cite the test (who, how many, how long), or describe the experience. |
| Guarantee ("satisfied or refunded", "lifetime guarantee") | High | Must exist with its conditions stated. Presenting rights the law already gives (such as the legal conformity guarantee) as a special feature of the offer is a banned practice (Directive 2005/29/EC, Annex I, point 10). | Quote the guarantee exactly, with conditions. |
| Testimonials, reviews, star ratings | High | Must be genuine; fake reviews and misrepresented reviews are banned (Annex I, points 23b–23c). | Use only supplied reviews, verbatim. |
| Price reductions ("-30 %", "was €49") | High | The reference price is the lowest price in the previous 30 days (Directive 98/6/EC, art. 6a). | Use only the brief's figures. |
| Urgency and scarcity ("today only", "last units") | High if not true | False limited-time or limited-stock claims are a banned practice (Annex I, point 7). | Use only the brief's deadlines and stock facts. |
| Origin ("Made in France", "fabriqué en France") | Medium–high | Must meet origin rules; misleading if the substantial transformation happened elsewhere. | Confirm, or name what is French ("designed in Paris"). |
| "Natural", "100 % natural", "pure", "clean" | Medium | No legal definition for most products; misleading if synthetic ingredients or heavy processing are involved. | Be specific: "98 % ingredients of natural origin (ISO 16128)", only if documented. |

## By sector

### Food and food supplements

Rules: Regulation (EC) 1924/2006 on nutrition and health claims; Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 on food information. They apply to labels, websites, ads and social posts.

- **Health claims:** only claims authorised in the EU Register (list in Regulation (EU) 432/2012), with their conditions of use and wording close to the authorised text.
- **Never allowed for food:** preventing, treating or curing a disease (1169/2011, art. 7(3)); the rate or amount of weight loss; suggesting health suffers without the product; recommendations by individual doctors (1924/2006, art. 12).
- **General well-being** ("good for you", "bien-être", "vitalité") must sit next to a specific authorised claim (1924/2006, art. 10(3)).
- **Botanicals:** most plant health claims have been "on hold" since 2010, neither authorised nor rejected. Some brands use on-hold claims under transitional rules at their own risk, and national practice varies. This calls for a written policy, signed off by a regulatory lawyer, not a case-by-case guess.
- **Red-flag words:** détox/detox, brûle-graisse/fat-burner, renforce l'immunité/boosts immunity (outside the authorised wording), anti-inflammatoire, anti-stress / apaise l'anxiété, guérit/cures, soigne/treats, prévient/prevents, anti-cholestérol, "cliniquement prouvé".
- **Safer routes:** the authorised wording when the conditions are met ("Vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system"), or sensory and ritual language ("a warm, spiced cup to close the day").

### Cosmetics and personal care

Rules: Regulation (EC) 1223/2009, art. 20; Regulation (EU) 655/2013 common criteria (legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making); the EU technical document on cosmetic claims.

- Presenting a product as treating or preventing a disease makes it a medicinal product by presentation (France: Code de la santé publique, L5111-1). High risk: "soigne l'eczéma", "traite l'acné", "cicatrisant", "anti-inflammatoire", "guérit".
- **Efficacy** ("anti-rides", "hydrate 24 h", "-30 % de rides en 4 semaines") needs tests held before publishing.
- **"Free from"** (sans parabènes, sans silicone) must not denigrate legally permitted ingredients or be the product's only selling point; "hypoallergenic" needs evidence.
- **"Bio" / organic cosmetics:** no EU regulation; use only with a certification held (COSMOS, Ecocert, Natrue) and that scheme's rules.

### Wellness services and therapies

- No promise to diagnose, treat or cure illness, and no suggestion to replace medical care. In France, diagnosis or treatment claims by non-medical practitioners risk both misleading practice and illegal practice of medicine.
- Describe the method, the experience, relaxation and well-being, and the practitioner's real training.
- **Meta ads:** the personal attributes policy bars asserting or implying the reader's health condition ("Struggling with anxiety?"). Speak about the offer, not the reader's condition.

### Environmental, natural and organic claims

- **Directive (EU) 2024/825**, applicable from 27 September 2026, bans: generic environmental claims ("eco-friendly", "green", "respectueux de la planète", "écologique", "climate-friendly") without recognised excellent environmental performance; claims of neutral, reduced or positive climate impact based on offsetting; sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or set by public authorities; whole-product claims that only concern a part.
- **France already bans** "biodégradable", "respectueux de l'environnement" and equivalent wording on products and packaging (loi AGEC, Code de l'environnement L541-9-1), and strictly limits carbon-neutrality claims (loi Climat et Résilience).
- **"Bio", "biologique", "organic"** on food and agricultural products are reserved for certified products (Regulation (EU) 2018/848).
- **Safer routes:** specific, verifiable facts ("bottle made from 100 % recycled glass", "recyclable cardboard box"), and certifications actually held, named.

### B2B, software and services

- ROI, time saved and performance figures need a documented source (case study, scope, period).
- Security and compliance claims ("RGPD-compliant", "ISO 27001", "bank-grade security") are high risk: they must be literally true, and certifications must be held.

## Ad platforms

Google Ads and Meta review ads against their own policies, which can be stricter than the law: unverified rankings and superlatives, health claims, before/after images and personal attributes can be disapproved. Check the platform's current policy before launch.
